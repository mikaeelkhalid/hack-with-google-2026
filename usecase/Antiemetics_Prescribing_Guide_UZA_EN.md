# Antiemetics — Prescribing Guide (UZA)

*English translation of "AImesis CINV Control – Handleiding voorschrijven van antiemetica UZA" (use case 3)*

Combination therapy is recommended for CINV (*chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting*). For determining emetogenicity and choosing therapy, refer to the separate guideline "Antiemetics Policy" (see separate document). Each drug is discussed individually below.

---

## 1. Ondansetron

Ondansetron is a 5-HT3 antagonist.

- **Indication:** first-choice antiemetic
- **Dose:** 5 mg/m² (max. 8 mg) 2–3×/day on chemotherapy days, up to and including 3 days after chemotherapy if needed.
- **Forms available in pharmacy:**
  - Ondansetron® 8 mg/4 ml ampoule IV
  - Ondansetron 2 mg/ml oral solution, 32 ml
  - Ondansetron® Teva 4 mg film-coated tablet
  - Ondansetron® 8 mg tablet
  - Zofran® Zydis 8 mg orodispersible tablet
- Ondansetron is included 2×/day by default in the ChemoPro schedules, but for hospitalised patients the agreement is that it is **also prescribed via the EHR**: 2×/day + 1 extra dose PRN (see also the antiemetics policy). This is not needed for day admissions.
- Ondansetron treatment is often continued at home; by default we recommend 2×/day up to and including 3 days after the last chemotherapy. Convert the IV order to oral at discharge so that the medication overview contains the correct information.
- **Caution:** ondansetron is **not reimbursed in community pharmacies**; patients must always collect it with a prescription from the hospital pharmacy! Always state the required quantity on the prescription.
- **Caution:** ondansetron 4 mg/2 ml ampoules are also available, but not every generic is reimbursed in the oncology setting! Therefore always prescribe **8 mg/4 ml ampoules**, even for a dose of 4 mg or less.

## 2. Alizapride

Alizapride is a dopamine receptor antagonist.

- **Indication:** second-choice antiemetic; used much less in the new CINV guidelines.
- **Dose:** 1 mg/kg 3–6×/day (max. 300 mg/day), IV on chemotherapy days.

  UZA rounding agreement for oral administration:

  | Body weight | Dose |
  |---|---|
  | < 12 kg | 1 mg/kg |
  | 12–20 kg | 15 mg |
  | 21–30 kg | 25 mg (= half a 50 mg tablet) |
  | 31–40 kg | 35 mg |
  | > 40 kg | 50 mg |

- **Forms available in pharmacy:**
  - Litican® 50 mg tablet
  - Litican® 50 mg/2 ml ampoule
  - Alizapride 15 mg capsules (compounded preparation)
  - Alizapride 35 mg capsules (compounded preparation)
- For highly emetogenic chemotherapy, alizapride can be combined with ondansetron, aprepitant and/or dexamethasone. See CINV guideline.
- For young children, IV ampoules may exceptionally be given orally (an opened ampoule keeps for 24 h in the refrigerator).
- Reimbursement of oral alizapride requires a certificate. For each new diagnosis, the physician must complete a non-specific reimbursement request form (available on the BCFI website).
- The validity of this certificate is limited to 6 months.
- We aim to prescribe Litican **only intravenously and only during chemotherapy**. We also no longer do this routinely for all chemotherapy, only for highly emetogenic or combination regimens. If Litican treatment is nevertheless continued orally (at home), always state the required quantity on the prescription and refer patients to the community pharmacy.
- Alizapride is **not available in the Netherlands**.

## 3. Dexamethasone

Dexamethasone acts on inflammatory mediators and has an additive effect in combination with 5-HT3 antagonists.

- **Indication:** antiemetic
- **Dose:**

  | BSA | Standard | In combination with Aprepitant® |
  |---|---|---|
  | < 0.6 m² | 2 mg/m² 2×/day IV or PO | 1 mg/m² 2×/day |
  | > 0.6 m² | 4 mg/m² 2×/day IV or PO | 2 mg/m² 2×/day |

- **Forms available in pharmacy:**
  - Dexamethasone 1 mg/ml suspension
  - Dexamethasone capsules (various strengths)
  - Aacidexam® 5 mg/ml (= dexamethasone 4 mg/ml)
- For highly emetogenic chemotherapy, dexamethasone can be added to therapy with ondansetron, alizapride and/or aprepitant; see antiemetics policy.
- **Caution:** if dexamethasone is combined with aprepitant or fosaprepitant (Ivemend®), the dexamethasone dose must be **halved**.
- The use of corticosteroids as antiemetics is **not recommended** in brain tumours, lymphomas and leukaemia.
- **Caution when switching from IV to PO:** the dose of Aacidexam® is expressed as a salt form and must therefore be converted to the active base. Aacidexam® contains 5 mg/ml dexamethasone sodium phosphate, which corresponds to an active dose of 3.8 mg/ml dexamethasone base (rounded to 4 mg/ml).

## 4. Aprepitant / Fosaprepitant

Aprepitant (and its prodrug fosaprepitant) is a neurokinin-1 (NK1) receptor antagonist.

- **Indication:** antiemetic for moderately and highly emetogenic chemotherapy
- **Dose:**

  **Oral (PO)**
  | Form / age | Day 1 | Days 2 and 3 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Capsules (> 12 y) | 125 mg | 80 mg |
  | Suspension (6 months – < 12 y) | 3 mg/kg (max. 125 mg) | 2 mg/kg (max. 80 mg) |

  **Intravenous (IV)**
  | Age | 3-day regimen | Or single dose (D1 only) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 6 months – < 2 y | D1: 3 mg/kg (max. 115 mg), then D2–D3: 2 mg/kg (max. 80 mg) | 5 mg/kg (max. 150 mg) |
  | 2 – < 12 y | D1: 3 mg/kg (max. 115 mg), then D2–D3: 2 mg/kg (max. 80 mg) | 4 mg/kg (max. 150 mg) |
  | ≥ 12 y | D1: 115 mg, then D2–D3: 80 mg | 150 mg |

- **Forms available in pharmacy:**
  - Aprepitant Teva® capsules 125 mg/80 mg/80 mg
  - Aprepitant suspension 20 mg/ml (compounded preparation)
  - Ivemend® 150 mg vial (in consultation with the pharmacy; to be ordered or as samples)
- Take orally approximately 1 hour before the start of chemotherapy on D1, D2 and D3. If chemotherapy is given on D1 only, the D2 and D3 doses may be given in the morning.
- For regimens with chemotherapy on five consecutive days, a dose of 80 mg (or 2 mg/kg) may also be continued on D4 and D5.
- **Caution:** if aprepitant or fosaprepitant is combined with dexamethasone, the dexamethasone dose must be **halved**.

### Ivemend® (fosaprepitant)
- If needed, Ivemend® (fosaprepitant, a prodrug of aprepitant) can be administered IV in consultation with the pharmacy.
- This drug is **not reimbursed**. It can sometimes be requested as samples.
- Reconstitute the vial with 5 ml NaCl 0.9%. Further dilute the dose in 100–250 ml NaCl 0.9%. Infusion time: children < 12 years over 60 minutes; children > 12 years over 30 minutes. Ensure the infusion is completed 30 minutes before the start of chemotherapy.
- Ivemend is **incompatible with cations** (Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺, …). Therefore do not give it together with hydration bags containing calcium or magnesium, nor with Ringer's or Hartmann's solutions.
- For one-day chemotherapy regimens, Ivemend can also be given as a single dose.

### Aprepitant suspension
- Emend suspension has been withdrawn from the market. Aprepitant suspension must therefore now be made as a compounded preparation.
- For admitted patients (and outpatient clinic contacts) you can prescribe it as "aprepitant suspension 20 mg/ml", as it is listed in our product database. For ambulatory contacts, or when registering home medication, it must be prescribed as a compounded preparation.
- No reimbursement or certificate option exists for the compounded preparation for children under 12. Each bottle of aprepitant will therefore be charged to the RIZIV/INAMI paediatric oncology convention.
- If chemotherapy is given on day 1 only in the day hospital, the bottle can be sent home with the patient to administer the day 2 and day 3 doses at home.

### Interactions
- **Caution:** aprepitant is a **CYP3A4 inhibitor**.
- The combination with **ifosfamide** increases the risk of neurotoxicity. If aprepitant is nevertheless needed, adequate monitoring of the patient for signs of neurotoxicity is recommended.

## 5. Olanzapine

Olanzapine is an atypical antipsychotic. Because of its effect on receptors involved in chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (mainly antagonism of serotonin and dopamine receptors), recent guidelines (POGO) put it forward as an antiemetic.

- **Indication:** prophylaxis and treatment of CINV; see antiemetics policy
- **Dose:**
  - < 2 years: no indication (no data in the literature)
  - ≥ 2 years: 0.1 mg/kg PO (max. 5 mg) 1×/day, from D1 up to and including 3 days after the last chemotherapy; round the dose **down** to the nearest 0.5 mg
- **Forms available in pharmacy:**
  - Zyprexa® 5 mg Velotab (= orodispersible tablet)
  - Olanzapine® Sandoz 5 mg scored (divisible) tablet
  - Olanzapine 1 mg capsules (= compounded preparation)
- Take the first oral dose approximately 1 hour before the start of chemotherapy, and thereafter preferably in the evening because of drowsiness.
- Only Olanzapine® Sandoz tablets can be split.
- **Never as monotherapy** for CINV; use with other antiemetics in highly emetogenic chemotherapy.
- If needed, olanzapine can also be prescribed as compounded capsules. There are no stability data for preparing a suspension.

## 6. Lorazepam

Benzodiazepines have a weak antiemetic effect but play an important role in **anticipatory** nausea and vomiting.

- **Indication:** antiemetic
- **Dose:** 0.025–0.05 mg/kg 3–4×/day (max. 1 mg per dose). A higher dose for adolescents may be considered individually.
- **Forms available in pharmacy:**
  - Temesta® Expidet 1 mg
  - Temesta® Expidet 2.5 mg
  - Temesta® 4 mg/ml ampoule
  - Lorazepam 1 mg/ml suspension
- Lorazepam can also be used for **breakthrough** nausea and vomiting if the established treatment with a 5-HT3 antagonist, Litican and dexamethasone is insufficient.
- If possible, round the dose to a 1 mg Expidet tablet, as this is often easier to take when vomiting.

## 7. Palonosetron

Palonosetron is a long-acting 5-HT3 antagonist.

- **Indication:** antiemetic when aprepitant is contraindicated and nausea is expected to be insufficiently controlled with ondansetron, Litican and dexamethasone
- **Dose:** age-dependent. UZA agreement:
  - < 16 years: no indication
  - ≥ 16 years: 250 mcg on D1, repeated on D4 if needed
- **Available form:** Palonosetron® 250 mcg/5 ml
- Palonosetron is given **once per cycle** as an intravenous bolus approximately 30 minutes before the first chemotherapy starts.
- If nausea is insufficiently controlled during multi-day chemotherapy, palonosetron can be given again after 72 hours (i.e. on day 4).
- **Caution:** palonosetron is a long-acting 5-HT3 antagonist and is **not combined with ondansetron during the first 3 days** after administration (duplicate medication).
- For information: the elimination half-life of palonosetron is 20–30 hours in children and 40 hours in adults.

---

## 8. Granisetron

- **Indication:** antiemetic as an alternative to ondansetron.
- **Dose:**
  - Oral: 40 mcg/kg every 12 hours, max. 2 mg/day
  - IV: 40 mcg/kg (max. 3 mg) as a single dose before chemotherapy, slowly over 10 minutes. One extra dose may be given within 24 h.
- **Availability in pharmacy:**
  - Kytril® 1 mg tablets
  - Kytril® 3 mg ampoule IV
- A small stock of Kytril is held in the UZA pharmacy; contact the pharmacy when starting.
- Granisetron is a 5-HT3 antagonist, like ondansetron. In some children one seems to work better than the other, so it may be a possible alternative.
- Granisetron can be combined with other antiemetics such as aprepitant or dexamethasone. It must of course **never be combined with ondansetron**.
- Granisetron is also **not reimbursed in community pharmacies** and must therefore be collected from the hospital pharmacy.
- Granisetron is a **CYP3A4 substrate**. Interactions are possible.

### Glossary of local terms
- **UZA** – Universitair Ziekenhuis Antwerpen (Antwerp University Hospital)
- **EPD** – electronic patient record (EHR)
- **QS / PRN** – as needed
- **Officina apotheek** – community (retail) pharmacy
- **Magistrale bereiding** – compounded (pharmacy-prepared) formulation
- **BCFI** – Belgian Centre for Pharmacotherapeutic Information
- **RIZIV / INAMI** – Belgian National Institute for Health and Disability Insurance
- **POGO** – Pediatric Oncology Group of Ontario
